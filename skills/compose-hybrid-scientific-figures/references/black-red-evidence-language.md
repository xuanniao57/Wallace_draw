# Black--red evidence language

Use this reference when the user requests a black/red scientific style or supplies a polished journal overview figure as a visual benchmark. Extract transferable grammar; do not copy journal branding, article text, icons, maps, or panel artwork.

## What makes the reference effective

The reference combines three levels without making them compete:

1. **Procedural spine.** A numbered vertical sequence makes the method readable before any small labels are inspected.
2. **Conceptual synthesis.** A large hub-and-orbit construction translates the linear procedure into the paper's governing idea.
3. **Empirical plate.** Repeated map, model-explanation, and representative-image rows show that the framework produces inspectable evidence.

The layout alternates between rectangular process containers and circular analytical motifs. Rectangles communicate ordered work; circles communicate integration, comparison, coverage, or clustering. This shape grammar is more important than ornament.

## Palette roles

| Layer | Default treatment | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | white | breathing room and page continuity |
| Structure | near-black text, rules, outlines, arrows | reading order, boundaries, ordinary relations |
| Thesis accent | deep or active red | focal construct, selected state, synthesis, heat/intensity, conclusion |
| Scope field | pale red at low opacity | shared analytical region, comparison envelope, or response space |
| Secondary evidence | muted grey or restrained auxiliary colors | categories or source imagery that require distinction |

Do not distribute red uniformly. Concentrate it where the paper's claim changes state: identification, comparison, integration, response, or final typology.

## Composition patterns

### Lane plus hub

Use a 40:60 or 45:55 split. Place the reproducible workflow in a narrow left lane and the explanatory synthesis in a larger right field. Align both to the same top and bottom anchors so they read as one figure.

### Numbered methodological spine

Place large numerals or phase markers in a gutter, separate from panel interiors. Keep each phase horizontally compact and connect phases with one continuous black rule. This creates order without boxing every detail.

### Focal field with orbiting evidence

Put one thesis-bearing construct in the center of a pale red field. Arrange three to five circular evidence windows around it. Use short radial labels and a small number of arrows. The center should summarize, not duplicate, the satellites.

### Paired result plate

For global/local, before/after, baseline/proposed, or two-scale comparisons, repeat the same geometry on both sides:

- identical map dimensions and extent logic;
- aligned model-diagnostic strips;
- aligned representative samples;
- one shared legend when possible;
- red only for the comparison thesis or highlighted samples.

Mirroring makes differences visible without explanatory prose.

### Evidence thumbnails

Use small maps, patches, charts, or image crops as proof tokens inside the workflow. Crop tightly, preserve their analytical content, and label them with one to four words. A thumbnail must carry evidence, not merely texture.

## Information-density control

- Preserve large white gutters between macro-regions even when local panels are dense.
- Use pale fills to bind related items instead of adding nested borders.
- Limit each micro-node to one noun phrase; move definitions and caveats to the caption.
- Repeat shapes, sizes, and alignment before adding decoration.
- Use one strong center of gravity per macro-region.
- Keep narrative callouts outside the analytical geometry: red heading, one short black explanation.

## Translation workflow

1. State the paper's thesis in one sentence and underline the state-changing nouns or verbs. Those are candidates for red.
2. Separate procedural steps, conceptual relations, and empirical outputs.
3. Choose a rectangular spine for procedure, circular motifs for synthesis, and repeated plates for comparison.
4. Compose in black and white first. Confirm reading order, grouping, and balance.
5. Add deep red to the thesis path and pale red to its shared scope.
6. Reintroduce only the auxiliary colors required by maps, clusters, divergent values, or source imagery.
7. Inspect at publication size and remove any label that merely narrates what the visual already proves.

## Failure modes

- **Pink everywhere:** the accent becomes wallpaper and the argument loses a focal path.
- **Dashboard fragmentation:** too many equally weighted cards erase the method-to-thesis hierarchy.
- **Decorative thumbnails:** tiny images add texture but no inspectable evidence.
- **Shape without semantics:** circles, rounded rectangles, and dashed lines are mixed without stable meaning.
- **False two-color purity:** forcing multi-class or signed data into red shades creates scientific ambiguity.
- **Reference imitation:** copying the article's exact geometry despite different evidence produces a visually similar but logically weak figure.

## Final test

At thumbnail size, a reader should see a black structural skeleton and one red thesis path. At reading size, they should be able to follow procedure, synthesis, and evidence without relying on a long caption.
